Seven months since then. Four of those spent suspended on a torture rack resetting my spine. Another two for rehab. 

Only one other made it out of the verdigris hellpits of Chaac Patera. And then he too was gone three weeks later. I'm sorry, I couldn't make it in time buddy.

The bald recruiter with the bespectacled beady eyes has them pegged on the leg, then my file, then my face, then quickly back to the file. Must have looked like hell. 
Something catches his eye; he stiffens his posture and his arm begins to snap to attention before he catches himself and coughs.

"First Loki Patera, Tarsus Regio, Prometheus, Uruk Sulcus, Second Loki Patera, Conamara Chaos, Argus Panoptes!" he whistles.  
His tone becomes somber again.
"Chaac Patera..."

He drops the papers.

"Sarge, what are you doing here?" He leans closer. "Someone put you to this?"

"Enlisting." 

"You know what I-  Look behind you, we're not short on backlog. A record like this, you should be living high on the hog on your VA benefits!"

Phantom pain pulsates through my mechanical knee as I wait in silence. 

"Uh... right. I'm sorry, but with *that*, you know I can't do anything for you but... You know Vulkan Security is always hiring. And there's always the mercs. With this record I'm sure you can skip the physical."

As I about-face, the Triskelion Lion of Nemean Company proudly bares its fangs swallowing Io, Europa, and Ganymede emblazoned on my cloak, commanding silence. 
Nothing is heard except the clack of my cane and boots. 

The show only lands harder because of the stairs. For a moment, I shift my weight onto a leg that belongs to me, and then my cheek digs hard into the red pavement. 
Turning over, it's watching me again. That mocking red eye of Jupiter. Well laugh damn you!

No one turns their head. People pass around me like a stream around stone. Good. A man who can't get up by himself is as good as dead on Io.

The boom times, when dad moved out to the Jovian moons volunteering for the Giant Leap Pioneer Project, are long over.
Our fathers' bubble dreams popped. The lost children of that era lived as sycophants, freeters, and burnouts.
Many dreamed, gazing bleary-eyed into bottles and gunbarrels - until the infestation of Io.

Infestation... Invasion... They called it many things like they knew what happened, but the truth is no one knows what the hell is going on.
Most called it The Revelation. End of the world. Whatever *it* is, it started in Argos, our pride in the fire sea of Loki Patera.

Something there made the poor bastards' topside suits bore into their bodies. Something changed their flesh into horrors of metal and muscle.
Chitin grew over their skin, giving them an insect-like appearance. Flesh stretched over servos.
Mechanical growths burst through their skulls, fusing into their helmets.

It gives them horns.

Some called them bugs, others bots.
But what they called them told you more about the person than about the abominations.

Nobody knows what caused it, but the rumor is it's the water.

Io used to be the driest object in the solar system, given it's basically one big angry volcano.
Europa has water, Io has a fire core. Every 3.5 days their orbits are closest.
Planetary mass railguns launch what the other needs towards each other in "the Kiss" or... the "Hump" for the "poetic" sort.
The first poor bastards to be infected were at the old capital, Argos. 

But, even in the end times, life goes on. It don't matter how good or bad things get, under that all-seeing eye, everyone's gotta make a buck. 
Or get drunk and punch someone.

Can't remember the first two bars, or was it three? I had to get it out of my system. I had to win, even if it was Pyrrhic.
It felt good, getting cracked with that ducking left hook. Both the bastard and I learned that the cane is not just for the aura. 

War is a fool's market, and whenever the bull comes to ride: sex, drink, and violence are the top sellers. 
And retards like me inevitably flow to Keno's gutter. Best damned dive bar in the new capital, Juno. 
Technically it's the "White Cow".
If you ask the barman why, Keno will give you the line:
"Io, one of Jupiter's harem. It's what he turned her into to hide her from his ever-jealous wife, Juno.
But everyone calls it after the owner, Captain Keno himself. 

Next thing I know, I'm nursing an old-fashioned, trying to endure the cacophony to no avail.

C: So was it loud? Like actually loud, or does it all just become white noise? 

She leans in closer and touches my arm. 

C: Is it true what they say about the smell? Say, how many people have you killed!?~

P: Lady, you've got no gun, no muscle, and no dictionary. What's a tart like you doing out here?

C: Content! 

I pull out my las-pistol and level it at her small chest.

P: This the content you were looking for?

She holds back a shriek and lets out something like a croak. 
Maybe a harsh lesson for such a young girl, but best to learn it now before she gets in *real* trouble.
I can see the sweat bead on her brow as she raises her hands. Her smile curdles, but she holds it together. 
She presses her ribcage into the cold steel.

The transformation bewilders my senses.

C: Oh, deary me! Chat, you see what rapscallions will do to a damsel out in these boondocks!? Constable! Call the thesaurus police!~

Eyes turn towards us. It's my turn to feel sweat on my brow. I move to put the gun away. 
Like a viper, she catches my hand, thrusts her slim arm over my elbow, and turns her tiny back into my chest. 
She holds my hand with the gun barrel up to her lips and snaps a selfie of her miming blowing smoke off of it.

C: See! I know how to shoot too, hotshot.

I push the goblin off me and she transfers the momentum into a spin and curtsies.

P: Get lost!

She doesn't. She slides back into the stool beside me like we've known each other for years, thumb already scrolling. 
On the Feed is the clip of us and our... dance. Notifications ring off her phone reading off donations and comments. 

C: Fifty. Seventy. Two-hundred! Oh~ Thank you chat! Sounds like drinks are on me today, old man. 

She gives a wink, taps in a bunch of cocktails on the touch monitor and begins to swipe her phone over it.

I grab her wrist. Not hard, just enough to stop her.  

P: You saying I can't pay for my own? _And I ain't an old man..._
C: I'm saying a *lady* sometimes feels generous. 
P: Generous ladies are dangerous. And guys pay for the first date. It's common sense.

She doesn't pull away and instead searches my face for something.

C: Old-fashioned, huh? But I don't mind that.

She plays with the word on her tongue and gnaws on the syllables with her teeth.

She lifts the martini glass and pours the will-o'-the-wisp down her gullet. Then the White Cow, and the Peacock, and the Gadfly.
She giggles.

Then she's up and gone - phone raised on a silly stick and already at the door. 

I turn to settle with old Keno in his vintage aloha shirt and prosthetic pince-nez working night shift. He has never raised his prices. Not since his own father's time.
Never gouging. That's the simple trick: fill the seats with every guttersnipe in the city. But only Keno gets it. 

He shakes his head.

"Already paid."

I shoot a glance back towards the door. She's smiling, waving her little phone like she's won something. The bright screen reads, in big letters. *~PAID~ ~PAID~ ~PAID~*

P: You little-!

I grab a bunch of old metal colonial chips from my pocket and slam them on the bar table.

"Hey! What!?-" 
He shrugs then rings a bell.
"Free round for everyone on the Nemean!"

Ex-Nemean, I think to myself.

The crowd erupts in roaring and hooting as I push past them. Lads in the corner slam their beers in rhythm and chant:

"THE THREE RETURNED! *ROAR* THE THREE RETURNED! *ROAR* THE THREE RETURNED!" *ROAR*

I push past the bouncer and, scanning, find no sign of the pink pixie.

A week later I run into her again. An alley in the slum quarters, and she's pulled a crowd. 
She's running the same bit this time, more practiced and bombastic. The idiot took the wrong lesson from our night. 

A bald thug with a neck of metal and plastic slaps the phone out of her hand. Before she can yelp, a punk with the Cassini Company emblem tattooed on his face comes up behind her and pins her arms in a Nelson.
The baldie retrieves the shattered phone, whips it around, and seeing the donations roll in, reads them aloud. Playing host.

Thug: Now, let's show you boys what you *really* came to see. 
The laughter pools like a bubbling sewer.

Before he even places a big paw on her top, the pings fire off wildly. The counter climbs higher and higher as she screams. 
None came to her rescue.

Instead, she sees before her a growing mass of lecherous eyes, mocking fangs, and lashing tongues.
He pulls hard.

I don't remember deciding. Three recoils. A pipe across my back. The heft of the tantalum cane through a neck, an ankle, and the gun level on a third.
Scatter.

I hobble to her and look down at her crumpled figure. She doesn't look up at me.
I unfasten my cloak and drape her in it. The phone, though broken, still rings out tallies of booty. I give it to her.
She snatches it away and holds it close to her chest as she starts to cry.
I hold her shoulders.

P: Don't do that.

She shakes her head, transmuting her pain into anger, and holds it in to a whimper. Good girl.

I look at the aftermath. Two burned-out craters in the chest of the fat bald one, one dead center in the forehead of the Cassini punk. 
Three more meteors for Io. I spit.

P: Not even the bugs do this.

P: I told you you were going to get killed pulling stunts like this. Unless you're in a safe crowd, no one's gonna save you, your little tricks won't work. 

P: The moon will eat you up and spit you out!

C: But... it did work though, didn't it?

I don't have an answer for that, but I feel a flash of anger. 

Airhead. Fresh meat. Dead by Wednesday. Too loud, too shining. If you stand out like that you'll only get hammered. Not my problem. 

I turn to leave but feel a tug on my cloak. 

I turn back to see her holding a gleaming glass badge of an eye. 

A Civilian War Journalism License. Anyone can film whatever they want on Io, no one can stop you.
One random voice on the Feed is just a curio, could be a scam, could be a rumor. 
But when the Ministry of Logistics and Public Relations sanctioned individuals to cover the plight on Io, it gives them authority.
Not power in politics or capital nor the barrel of a gun, but something... softer, more subtle. 

The power of faith - of legitimacy and belief. 

C: I know what you want. I know people like you. You're a dead man! 

...
There's something strange about this girl that makes me forget...

Like Cicero, she thrusts a hand to the heavens, stretching the full length of her slender fingers and one splayed palm at her solar plexus. 
My cloak flows around her, baring all, giving her the bearing of a divine nymph as the artificial rain beads on her alabaster skin.
I can't look away. 

She gesticulates with uncanny precision. For a brief moment nothing exists but us: theatre and audience. 

C: You want to go back out there, right? Because you are only alive out there among the corpses. 
C: Heh. Bodies don't even rot on Io; you live forever out there! 
C: You boys think you're so clever and romantic!

Like a figure skater, she closes her sad doe eyes and squeezes her tiny arms into her core. Then she extends her hand with a desperate smile.

C: I can give it to you.

An acid storm flashes outside the dome, and the interior sprinklers start mirroring it. 
For a split second half her face takes the appearance of a skull... Or am I seeing things?

P: Dangerous...

But I don't mind that either. 

I pull the cloak over her soaked body. She flushes and her hand trembles.

I accept it.


Our contract: I keep her breathing and she brings me out there and in the bacon. We cut it all fifty-fifty, profit and expense. 
Death clause: All the belongings of the deceased transfer to the surviving member - after a Space Vultures Guild investigation confirms no foul play.

For once in a long while, the path is clear if not bright:

*Stay alive and elbow out the competition. 
Those are the rules of the War Idols.*

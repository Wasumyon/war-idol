Seven months since then. Four of those spent suspended on a torture rack resetting my spine. Another two for rehab. 

Only three others made it out of the verdigris hellpits of Chaac Patera. And then one too was gone three weeks later. 
I'm sorry, buddy. I could have been there but... The brass didn't want me. Is it just the leg?
Anyway.

"First Loki Patera, Tarsus Regio, Prometheus, Uruk Sulcus, Second Loki Patera, Conamara Chaos, Argus Panoptes!" he whistles.

"Uh... right. I'm sorry, but with *that*, you know I can't do anything for you but... You know Vulkan Security is always hiring. And there's always the mercs. With this record I'm sure you can skip the physical."

I about-face. The cloak swings with me, and across it, the Triskelion Lion of Nemean Company proudly bares its fangs, swallowing Io, Europa, and Ganymede. It commands silence.
The only sound in the room is the clack of my cane, all the way to the door.

The show only lands harder because of the stairs. For a moment, I shift my weight onto a leg that belongs to me, and then my cheek digs hard into the red pavement. 
Turning over, it's watching me again. That mocking red eye of Jupiter. Well, laugh, damn you!

No one turns their head. People pass around me like a stream around stone. Good. A man who can't get up by himself is as good as dead on Io.

But, even in the end times, life goes on. It don't matter how good or bad things get, under that all-seeing eye, everyone's gotta make a buck. 
Or get drunk and punch someone.

# "Only three others made it out ... I'm sorry, buddy. I could have been there
#  but... The brass didn't want me. Is it just the leg? / Anyway."
#   YOUR FINAL SHAPE, and it beats the version with "Anyway." deleted, for a
#   mechanical reason: the shrug finally has something to deflect from. Put after a
#   helpless apology it read as coldness about a death; put after a fact with an
#   agent in it, the evasion has stakes.
#
#   THREE MOVES IN ONE BEAT, and the line breaks ARE the beats:
#     1. the fact      "The brass didn't want me."
#     2. the doubt     "Is it just the leg?"
#     3. the evasion   "Anyway."
#
#   The question does not confirm either reading, it ASKS. And asking beats silence,
#   because a doubt the reader is handed is a doubt they carry, where an absence has
#   to be noticed. It also makes him admit the unflattering option - he is not only
#   paranoid, he is considering that he is merely broken.
#
#   Why "Anyway." survives in THIS medium: a VN line is a held beat, so a shrug is
#   audible in a way a paragraph break is not. And it keeps the evasion HIS rather
#   than the author's cut to the next scene.
#
#   AND IT SETS UP A MISDIRECTION. The recruiter answers the question without
#   answering it - "with *that*, you know I can't do anything for you" - so the
#   mundane reading appears to be CONFIRMED twenty lines later by a bureaucrat. That
#   is the answer the reader will accept, which is what makes it a good false floor.
#
#   THE REASON IS NOT ON THE PAGE AND MUST NOT BE. The page says only "the brass
#   didn't want me" and stops. What they suspect, what the suit shows, and why
#   nobody would believe him are recorded in canon.md, not here.
#
#   THE THREE-DOOR LADDER. The chapter refuses him twice and then sells him a
#   substitute: the brass refuse him the war (this line); the recruiter refuses him
#   the army ("Sarge, what are you doing here?" / "Someone put you to this?"); and
#   the Ministry then sells him a licence. This line is the first rung, so the reader
#   walks into the office already knowing he has been refused once, and the scene
#   reads as the SECOND refusal.
#
#   BUT THE RECRUITER IS INNOCENT, and both readings have to stay alive:
#     - He goes solemn at "Chaac Patera..." because HE KNOWS IT WAS A MASSACRE. Not
#       suspicion - grief. Chaac is public knowledge and a byword for a catastrophe,
#       which is why the record recital is impressive right up to the last entry and
#       then kills the tone and the papers hit the desk. So the office must NOT read
#       as nervous. It reads as SORRY. Any hint of suspicion in this scene destroys
#       the ambiguity and turns the chapter into a conspiracy.
#     - "Someone put you to this?" is therefore concern, not suspicion: who talked
#       you into enlisting in this state. It only LOOKS like suspicion in hindsight,
#       which is the misdirection doing its work inside a single line.
#
#   TWO refusals make a pattern, and they are different KINDS: the brass refuse him
#   the war (ambiguous), the recruiter refuses him the army (mundane, sincere). Do not
#   add a third inside the intro, and never let the office confirm either reading.

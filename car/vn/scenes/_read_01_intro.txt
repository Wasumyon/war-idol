WAR IDOLS

Seven months since then. Four of those spent suspended on a torture rack resetting my spine. Another two for rehab.

Only three others made it out of the verdigris hellpits of Chaac Patera. And then one too was gone three weeks later.
I'm sorry, buddy. I could have been there but... The brass didn't want me. Is it just the leg?
Anyway.

The bald recruiter with the bespectacled, beady eyes has them pegged on the leg, then my file, then my face, then quickly back to the file. Must have looked like hell.
Something snags his eye; he stiffens his posture and his arm begins to snap to attention before he catches himself and coughs.

"First Loki Patera, Tarsus Regio, Prometheus, Uruk Sulcus, Second Loki Patera, Conamara Chaos, Argus Panoptes!" he whistles.
His tone becomes somber again.
"Chaac Patera..."

He drops the papers.

"Sarge, what are you doing here?" He leans closer. "Someone put you to this?"

"Enlisting."

"You know what I- Look behind you, we're not short on backlog. A record like this, you should be living high on the hog on your VA benefits!"

"You know what they're going to do to Io once we have the sky? Terraformers. A real port. Water rights. Courts. Kids born here who never see a suit."
He hears it come out of his mouth.
"It's the brochure. It's still true."

Phantom pain pulsates through my mechanical knee as I wait in silence.

"Uh... right. I'm sorry, but with that, you know I can't do anything for you but... You know Vulkan Security is always hiring. And there's always the mercs. With this record I'm sure you can skip the physical."

I about-face. The cloak swings with me, and across it, the Triskelion Lion of Nemean Company proudly bares its fangs, swallowing Io, Europa, and Ganymede. It commands silence.
The only sound in the room is the clack of my cane, all the way to the door.

The show only lands harder because of the stairs. For a moment, I shift my weight onto a leg that belongs to me, and then my cheek digs hard into the red pavement.
Turning over, it's watching me again. That mocking red eye of Jupiter. Well, laugh, damn you!

No one turns their head. People pass around me like a stream around stone. Good. A man who can't get up by himself is as good as dead on Io.

But, even in the end times, life goes on. It don't matter how good or bad things get, under that all-seeing eye, everyone's gotta make a buck.
Or get drunk and punch someone.

Can't remember the first two bars - or was it three? I had to get it out of my system. I had to win, even if the win was Pyrrhic.
It felt good, getting cracked with that gazelle hook. Both the bastard and I learned that the cane is not just for the aura.
It was the first thing all year that felt like anything.

The scare arrived with the beasts. Something in the water. Prices moved before anyone proved a thing.
Assured water, in the Ministry's bottles, at the Ministry's price. Behind it, the other market: old tanks, bench purifiers, whatever works without a stamp on it.
Rent on the new capital. Water by the litre.
A leg the VA replaces every four years, in the version they approved, at the price they set.
The good version I bought off a bench in the slums, and it's the one I walk on. The water comes from the same bench.
And the tax, every five days, alive or otherwise.

War is a fool's market, and whenever the bull comes to ride: sex, drink, and violence are the top sellers.
And retards like me inevitably flow to Keno's gutter. Best damned dive bar in the new capital, Juno.
A rat with a service record, drinking with the ones who never went.
Technically it's the "White Cow."
If you ask the barman why, Keno will give you the line:
"Io, one of Jupiter's harem. It's what he turned her into to hide her from his ever-jealous wife, Juno.
But everyone calls it after the owner, Captain Keno himself.

Next thing I know, I'm nursing an old-fashioned, trying to endure the cacophony to no avail.

Above the bar the Feed runs the war on mute. The same footage, every hour, from every angle.
A suit comes open along the spine and something stands up out of it wearing the parts.
The crawl underneath calls it the Revelation. It calls them the Horned.
Some say bugs. Some say bots. I reckon the word says more about the man than about the beasts.

It gives them horns.

"So was it loud? Like actually loud, or does it all just become white noise?"

She leans in closer and touches my arm.

"Is it true what they say about the smell? Do the Horned feel it - when it comes through? Say, how many people have you killed!?~"

"Lady, you've got no gun, no muscle, and no dictionary. What's a tart like you doing out here?"

"Content!"

I pull out my las-pistol and level it at her small chest.

"This the content you were looking for?"

She holds back a shriek and lets out something like a croak.
Maybe a harsh lesson for such a young girl, but best to learn it now before she gets in real trouble.
I can see the sweat bead on her brow as she raises her hands. Her smile curdles, but she holds it together.
She presses her ribcage into the cold steel.

The transformation bewilders my senses.

"Oh, deary me! Chat, you see what rapscallions will do to a damsel out in these boondocks!? Constable! Call the thesaurus police!~"

Eyes turn towards us. It's my turn to feel sweat on my brow. I move to put the gun away.
Like a viper, she catches my hand, thrusts her slim arms over my elbow, and turns her back into my chest.
She holds my hand with the gun barrel up to her lips and snaps a selfie of her miming blowing smoke off it.

"See! I know how to shoot too, hotshot."

I push the goblin off me and she transfers the momentum into a spin and curtsies.

"Get lost!"

She doesn't. She slides back into the stool beside me like we've known each other for years, thumb already scrolling.
On the Feed is the clip of us and our... dance. Notifications ring out of her phone as it reads donations and comments.

"Fifty. Seventy. Two-hundred! Oh~ Thank you chat! Sounds like drinks are on me today, old man."

She gives a wink, taps in a bunch of cocktails on the touch monitor and begins to swipe her phone over it.

I grab her wrist. Not hard, just enough to stop her.

"You saying I can't pay for my own? and I ain't an old man..."
"I'm saying a lady sometimes feels generous."
"Generous ladies are dangerous. And guys pay for the first date. It's common sense."

She doesn't pull away and instead searches my face for something.

"Old-fashioned, huh? But I don't mind that."

She plays with the word on her tongue and gnaws on the syllables with her teeth.

She lifts her martini glass and pours the will-o'-the-wisp down her gullet. Then the White Cow, and the Peacock, and the Gadfly.
She giggles.

Then she's up and gone - phone raised on a silly stick and already at the door.

I turn to settle with old Keno in his vintage aloha shirt and prosthetic pince-nez working night shift. He has never raised his prices. Not since his own father's time.
Never gouging. That's the simple trick: fill the seats with every guttersnipe in the city. But only Keno gets it.

He shakes his head.

"Already paid."

I shoot a glance back towards the door. She's smiling, waving her little phone like she's won something. The bright screen reads, in big letters. ~PAID~ ~PAID~ ~PAID~

"You little-!"

I grab a bunch of old metal colonial chips from my pocket and slam them on the bar table.

"Hey! What!?-"
He shrugs then rings a bell.
"Free round for everyone on the Nemean!"

Ex-Nemean, I think to myself.

The crowd erupts in roaring and hooting as I push past them. Lads in the corner slam their beers in rhythm and chant:

"THE THREE RETURNED! ROAR THE THREE RETURNED! ROAR THE THREE RETURNED!" ROAR

It was four, damn it.
But never let the numbers fuck up a good chant.

I push past the bouncer and, scanning, find no sign of the pink pixie.

A week later I run into her again. An alley in the slum quarters, and she's pulled a crowd.
She's running the same bit this time, more practiced and bombastic. The idiot took the wrong lesson from our night.

A thug with a neck of metal and plastic slaps the phone out of her hand. Before she can yelp, a punk with the Cassini Company emblem tattooed on his face comes up behind her and pins her arms in a Nelson.
The thug retrieves the shattered phone, whips it around, and seeing the donations roll in, reads them aloud. Playing host.

"Now, let's show you boys what you really came to see."
The laughter pools like a bubbling sewer.

Before he even places a big paw on her top, the pings fire off wildly. The counter climbs higher and higher as she screams.
None came to her rescue.

Instead, she sees before her a growing mass of lecherous eyes, mocking fangs, and lashing tongues.
He pulls hard.

I don't remember deciding. Three recoils. A pipe across my back. The heft of the tantalum cane through a neck, an ankle, and the gun level on a third.
Scatter.

I hobble to her and look down at her crumpled figure. She doesn't look up at me.
I unfasten my cloak and drape her in it. The phone, though broken, still rings out tallies of booty. I give it to her.
She snatches it away and holds it close to her chest as she starts to cry.
I hold her shoulders.

"Don't do that."

She shakes her head, transmuting her pain into anger, and holds it down. Only a whimper gets out. Good girl.

I look at the aftermath. Two burned-out craters in the chest of the fat one, one dead center in the forehead of the Cassini punk.
Three more meteors for Io. I spit.

"Not even the bugs do this."

"I told you that you'd get killed pulling stunts like this. Unless you're in a safe crowd, no one's gonna save you, your little tricks won't work."

"The moon will eat you up and spit you out!"

"But... it did work though, didn't it?"

I don't have an answer for that, but I feel a flash of anger.

Airhead. Fresh meat. Dead by Wednesday. Too loud, too shiny. If you stand out like that you'll only get hammered. Not my problem.

I turn to leave but feel a tug on my cloak.

I turn back to see her holding a gleaming glass badge of an eye.

A Civilian War Journalism License. Anyone can film whatever they want out here. No one can stop them.
The stamp is what turns a voice into the news.
The Ministry of Logistics and Public Relations.

I could have gone with the mercs. Vulkan would have taken me - skip the physical, no questions.
A merc's contract is his own. Nobody's stamp on it. Nobody's name but his.

I wanted a name on mine.

Something with a desk behind it. Something that says I'm allowed.

The power of faith, I suppose.
Of legitimacy and belief.
Authority you don't get to check.

"I know what you want. I know people like you. You're a dead man!"

...
There's something strange about this girl that makes me forget...

Suddenly, she looks up at me. Then, as if she were Cicero addressing the senate, she lengthens through her fingers towards Jupiter, the other splayed hand laid at her solar plexus. The tendons cord the back of her hand.
My cloak falls open off her as if in slow motion as she moves. She doesn't notice. The artificial rain beads on her alabaster skin.
She has the bearing of Bernini's Daphne, and... something older underneath it that I haven't got a name for.
I can't look away.

She gesticulates with uncanny precision. For a brief moment nothing exists but us: theatre and audience. Tears well at the sides of her eyes. Are these artificial too?
No.

They're real.

"You want to go back out there, right? Because you are only alive out there among the corpses."
"Heh. Bodies don't even rot on Io; you live forever out there!"
"You boys think you're so clever and romantic!"

Like a figure skater, she closes her sad doe eyes and squeezes her arms into her core. Then she extends her hand with a desperate smile.

"I can give it to you."

An acid storm flashes outside the dome, and the interior sprinklers start mirroring it.
For a split second half her face takes the appearance of a skull... Or am I seeing things?

Io has no water. The storm is acid, and what beads on the glass is bought and piped in - and nobody can tell you where it came from.

"Dangerous..."

But I don't mind that either.

I pull the cloak over her soaked body. She flushes and her hand trembles.

I accept it.

Our contract: I keep her breathing and she brings me out there and in the bacon. We cut it all fifty-fifty, profit and expense.
Death clause: All the belongings of the deceased transfer to the surviving member - after a Space Vultures Guild investigation confirms no foul play.

For once in a long while, the path is clear if not bright:

Stay alive and elbow out the competition.
Those are the rules of the War Idols.

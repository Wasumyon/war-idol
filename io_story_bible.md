# War Idol — Story Bible

**What this file owns:** the big picture — premise, theme, the twist, who the main characters are
and where they go, Albrecht, the acts, the endings. Anything you would say when pitching the story.

**What it does not own:** the detailed rulings a scene must obey. Those live in `canon.md` (an
index) and `canon/NN_*.md`. **Every fact lives in exactly one file. If the bible and canon
disagree, the bible wins** and canon gets fixed.

Inspired by *The Catcher in the Rye* and *Legend of the Galactic Heroes*.

---

## The spine

> **The ministry's logistics brought the infection, and the ministry's logistics sells the licence
> that authenticates the war against the infected.**

Every element serves the licence. If a beat does not touch this, it is decoration.

---

## Core concept and theme

* **The dehumanisation of war.** A sci-fi allegory of how the military and media complexes sanitise
  state violence.
* **The unreliable narrator as a survival mechanism.** The protagonist copes with what he did on the
  battlefield through trauma, denial, and a military-grade AR filter.
* **The anti-stagnation dilemma.** A painful, horrific truth against a grand utilitarian lie that
  claims to save humanity from existential decay.
* **Tone.** Dark humour from the gap between viewers with 24/7 access to the war and the people
  actually fighting it.

---

## World and setting

* **Io** — a hostile, half-terraformed moon choking on sulfur haze. Life support is visibly
  decaying; society is crippled by institutional corruption, depression and ennui. A junta holds it
  under martial law, and a separatist movement fights back. Detail: `canon/09_places.md`,
  `canon/12_physics.md`, `canon/03_water.md`.
* **The cosmic plan** — Albrecht's blueprint is to send Io into Europa. The collision destroys both
  hostile worlds and, over a few generations, fuses them into one permanently habitable **Eden**.

---

## The lie

* **There is no pathogen and no alien.** The infestation is a fabrication.
* **The enemy are just humans** — the lower-class workforce: miners, terraformers, hard-rock
  labour, in industrial exosuits, cybernetic prosthetics and bulky hazard gear. Nothing has fused,
  grown or turned. Their bodies are ordinary.
* **The visor makes the monster.** Military AR visors, issued through the Ministry's logistics
  branch, render those people as feral, 9-foot insectoid-robo-human mutants: the gear scaled up and
  distorted, plating as chitin, fittings as horns.
  * *Audio:* human screaming and radio static become synthesised roars.
  * *Visuals:* interface lag shifts anchors and hitboxes — a subtle hint that the 3D monster is
    covering a smaller human frame.
* Some of Albrecht's old loyalists are among them, caught in the crossfire (see Albrecht).
* Scene rules for writing the lie — the tells, who has seen what: `canon/02_the_lie.md`.

## Characters

### The narrator (MC)
* A disabled war veteran, maimed at **Chaac Patera**, turned frontline camera assistant and
  security for a streamer. The player names him at New Game; Chip's nicknames for him are
  **Meathead** / **Blockhead**.
* **Motive he admits to: boredom.** Nothing at home can kill him or make him care, so she sells him
  a calling. **The trauma is real** but not a motive he can see.
* **Phantom pain is the tell.** His missing leg flares when he gets close to the truth of what he
  saw. When the visor or his own mind glosses over something, the body answers for it.
* **A legitimist.** He wants a licence, a stamp, a name — legitimacy — and the Ministry
  manufactures and sells exactly that. Detail: `canon/04_narrator.md`.

### Chip
* A VTuber off a failing adult-content page who partners with him. She brings the charisma; he
  keeps her alive; they split 50/50. She sells and never confesses. Oblivious, obnoxious and
  harmless, desensitised to death at first, slowly growing sympathetic to the soldiers.
* She is the story's ideological foil in Act 3. Detail: `canon/05_chip.md`.

### Albrecht — the antagonist and fallen reformer
* **Backstory, in order:**
  1. A charismatic political and military leader who pushed the Europa–Io collision plan.
  2. Purged by corrupt elites. He surrendered rather than start a civil war, and was demoted to a
     **logistics officer** — supply chains, gear fabrication, the visor filters.
  3. From that invisible desk he built **the visor twist** and used it to stage a **coup**.
  4. Most of the other functionaries died. He took **Grand Executor** from the remnants.
  5. Not everyone is all right with this, but in the chaos his results speak for themselves.
* **His loyalists are in the crossfire.** Some who supported him stayed at their posts and are
  caught in his war. He could not openly manoeuvre to save them without telegraphing the coup. It is
  a cost he accepted, not a purge he ordered.
* **Motive: utilitarian mercy.** He built the monster filter so his soldiers would not collapse
  while carrying out a triage campaign he believes is necessary.
* **Form:** the Grand Inquisitor. He argues rather than enforces, and wants agreement rather than
  obedience. Title usage and his voice: `canon/13_authority.md`.

### The other streamers
Four streamers, one per side of how a culture consumes a war:
* **Chip** — above.
* **Langley** — the only one who talks to soldiers as a soldier. A bad interviewer who bonds through
  shared experience. Mercs off-rotation. Believes her missing father is alive.
* **Aysel** — Chip's rival; a gyaru idol and grifter propagandist with inflated kill counts who
  cheers on sacrifice. A cute chibi avatar over cruelty; secretly an ex-child soldier. Albrecht's
  coup wiped out her family and she blames the bugs. She doesn't know it was Albrecht until her
  own EMP trap for the team knocks out the AR. **Then she turns** and joins the team for a while.
  Detail: `canon/08_cast.md`.
* **Kathay** — an underground conspiracy broadcaster with real hacking skills; bombastic, talks over
  guests, sells supplements and Faraday helmets. After Ottenok dies she takes over his position as
  the info dealer — badly.

Supporting cast — Tank, Keno, the moon-touched soldier, the recruiter and others: `canon/08_cast.md`.

## Structure — three acts

The campaign runs **40 in-game days**, with checkpoints every five. Loop and economy:
`canon/11_campaign.md` and `vn_game_design.md`. Beat-by-beat order: `outline.md`.

### Act 1: The protective illusion (0–25%)
* Establish the gritty reality of Io and the popular worship of Albrecht through money, pamphlets
  and soundbites.
* **The inciting red flag:** a moon-touched homeless veteran raves that the monsters are just men.
  The MC shoves money into his hand to silence him — and gets a violent spike of phantom pain.

### Act 2: The cracking facade (25–75%)
* The MC and Chip push too deep into a combat zone for views and get cornered. **Albrecht personally
  intervenes to rescue them**, earning loyalty and dropping cryptic, double-meaning hints.
* **Mid-game climax (70–75%):** Aysel springs an EMP trap on them and is caught in it herself.
  The AR filter crashes mid-combat, and the MC is in a raw physical struggle with an ordinary human
  miner wielding industrial tools. **They are captured** at once by Albrecht's elite forces to
  contain the leak.

### Act 3: The interrogation and the fork (75–100%)
* Albrecht confronts them face to face — the numbers, the elites' corruption, and the plan to smash
  the moons into Eden. This is his one scene of self-justification.
* **The permanent fork:**
  * **Route A — Whistleblower, the burden of truth (canon route, full Act 3).** The player rejects
    Albrecht. The last act is **the heist**: with Chip, break out, get the hard, knock-down evidence,
    and escape with it under separatist support, then broadcast the unfiltered footage. Society
    collapses, the elites are exposed, the genocide stops, and humanity has to face its stagnation.
    Aysel survives as a beacon, a possible next Grand Executor.
  * **Route B — Complicit, the hero (short epilogue).** The player agrees with Albrecht. A chilling
    time-skip: the MC lives a lavish, decorated life as a war hero; Chip's channel is a huge success
    and her avatar's eyes are dead; the artificial war quietly grinds on toward Eden.
    **Aysel becomes the enemy** (rough, subject to change). The player may kill her. If they
    refuse, Albrecht moves to kill her, and the player may stop him. Each outcome changes the
    epilogue.
* **How the run feeds the fork is open** — `vn_game_design.md`, "The ending".

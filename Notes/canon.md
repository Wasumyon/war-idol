# War Idol — canon

Settled facts. `style_guide.md` holds the **writing rules**; this file holds the
**continuity facts**. If a line contradicts something here, one of the two is wrong,
and fixing it is a decision rather than a tweak.

Add here only when a fact is **decided**. Proposals belong in `01_intro_punchlist.md`
or the FLAGGED block at the foot of the relevant draft.

---

## 1. The spine

> **The ministry's logistics brought the infection, and the ministry's logistics sells
> the licence that authenticates the war against the infected.**

Every element serves the licence. If a beat does not touch this, it is decoration.

---

## 2. The lie — the biggest fact

**There is no pathogen.** No virus, no nanomachine, no "redemption" agent, no
water-borne cause. **The infestation is a fabrication.**

**CORRECTED — and it matters what the lie is.** *The lie is not "they are monstrous." The
lie is "they are not human."*

- The colonists **appear** human-machine **fused**, genuinely and horribly, and the doc's own
  touchstones are body horror for a reason: the Beast, the Infested Terran. Bodies welded
  into rigs, suits that no longer come off, prosthetics grown over. **The horror the reader
  sees is real.** None of the imagery is a trick.
- What the visor invents is the **species**. It takes a fused human and files it as vermin.
  So the machine does not manufacture the monstrosity — it **dehumanises** it.
- Therefore **the reader is never cheated.** Everything they saw was true and the *category*
  was false. Their revulsion is legitimate; their contempt is the crime. They were right to
  be horrified and wrong to think *bug*.
- **This is a better trap than "it was all a rendering"**, which risks feeling like a cheat.
  And the doc already says it: "the HUD renders the original human colonists as insectoid
  aliens." The *aliens* are the invention. The bodies are not.

**What is real**
- The colonists are **heavily cybernetic**, for practical reasons — Io is a hard-rock
  industrial colony. The metal is real, and so is the flesh it has gone into. (Why that is
  not a moral failing: style guide §5 on euphemism, and the note that "purity of the body"
  is a bourgeois concern.)
- A **military junta** holds Io under martial law, and it wants the moon.
- A **separatist** movement exists and fights.

**What is false**
- The **species**. Not the horror. The bodies are that bad; the *category* is manufactured.

**The visor is where the lie lives.** AR visor suits are regulated by the colonial
government and now issued through the junta's **logistics branch**. Its HUD renders
human colonists as **insectoid aliens** — the doc's own word, which is where *Sectoid*
comes from.

Consequences, and they bind every scene:
- **Nobody has ever seen a beast with their own eyes.** Every sighting is through
  Ministry hardware. If a character claims otherwise, it is a lie or a broken visor,
  and either way it is a scene.
- **The horns are a HUD artefact.** The engineer's round, toothed pauldrons are the
  standing example of what the visor misreads.
- **"No living colonist has been found"** (Langley, Day 3) is not a mystery. It is the
  proof. The colonists are the aliens.
- The war is therefore a **counterinsurgency sold as a xenocide**, and
  *the Revelation* is a manufactured apocalypse — which is why the Feed sells
  *the Apocalypse*.

## 3. Water

- Io has no water; Europa has excess. The railguns trade water for Io's sulfur, iron
  and SF6 **when the orbits align**, and the transfer is ***the Kiss*** officially and
  ***the Hump*** everywhere else.
- **The word arrives first as an ingredient.** It rides the water into civilian life, so a
  bar card lists *the Kiss* the way it lists sugar — *a spoon, sugar, and the Kiss in a
  scrambler*. The bar names and never glosses, which is the rule anyway: the trade is
  learned on the field, the first time a pillar is witnessed.
- **The water caused nothing.** The rumour that it did is **Ministry marketing**, and
  the marketing is what created the scare.
- **Assured water** — the Ministry's certified brand, in its own bottles, at its own
  price. The Ministry does not sell water. It sells **certification**. This is the
  licence in liquid form, one rung down: the player's daily proof of concept before
  anyone names the big idea.
- **The scare is the inflation.** Prices moved before anything was proved. That is why
  life is hard for the narrator and Chip — their bills are a war cost, not bad luck.
- The counter-market: old tanks, bench purifiers, gear that works **without a stamp on
  it**.

## 4. The narrator

- **The triad: practicality / morality / legitimacy.** Three *claimants*, not two poles
  and a tiebreak. He cannot reconcile the first two himself, so he subcontracts the job
  to legitimacy — **which makes it absolution.**
- **Legitimacy is a product**, and that is his tragedy: the Ministry manufactures and
  sells the very thing he believes is sacred. The recruiter sells a version that pays
  out after he is dead; he wants one that pays out in the hand. Same currency,
  different maturity.
- **He is a legitimist** — a snob about **paperwork**, never about people. He buys his
  leg and his water off a bench in the slums and rates both. He is never cruel to the
  competent.
- **Don Quixote.** He reaches for the big word and half-misses — *"I suppose"*,
  *"I reckon"*. He has pulls toward things beyond his grasp, in reality and in
  conception.
- **Boredom is the engine.** He goes back out because nothing at home can kill him or
  make him care. Not trauma, not revenge. That is why the pitch lands: she sells him a
  **calling**.
- **The debt, and the doubt.** The intro says only: *"I'm sorry, buddy. I could have been
  there but... / The brass didn't want me."* Two plants in one line, and neither is
  explained on the page.
  - **The debt:** guilt for a **deed**, not grief over an event. He *tried* to go back and
    was refused. The confession belongs to a later scene.
  - **The doubt — and it may be no more than the injury.** Two readings, and the page must
    support both while confirming neither:
    1. **The mundane one, and it is the one on the page:** he has one leg. The recruiter says
       it out loud — "with *that*" — and nobody ever says anything else.
    2. **His private one:** the state of his suit is the problem, and the brass suspect he saw
       too much at Chaac.
    **The overlap is the point.** Procedure is the Ministry's camouflage, and "medical" is the
    perfect alibi precisely because it is also true. That is the same shape as the visor: a
    real, mundane mechanism producing a lie with nobody lying. So the first beat of the intro
    quietly demonstrates the method the twist later runs on.
  - **And the alibi is the event itself.** Chaac was a real massacre and it is public
    knowledge — a byword. So the brass never have to lie about why they will not have him
    back: a genuine catastrophe covers any refusal they choose to give. That is the
    Ministry's method in its purest form, and it happens on the first page.
  - **Never confirm which it is — not even inside his own head.** The text says only *"The
    brass didn't want me."* and stops. The reader will supply the leg for themselves. And the
    reader should be able to suspect he is telling himself a flattering story, because
    **being persecuted is easier to live with than being broken**, and a man who would rather
    be a marked man than a cripple is sadder and far more interesting than either.
  - **And believing him solves nothing.** Nobody would believe him anyway — which is true, and
    which makes his silence rational rather than noble.
  - Consequence for the recruiter scene: *"Sarge, what are you doing here?"* and *"Someone
    put you to this?"* were already alarm, and now they read as a man who knows something.
    And *"you should be living high on the hog on your VA benefits"* stops being kindness
    and becomes a **settlement**. The pension is hush money, and the water scare ate it.
  - **Which is what the licence is actually for.** He already has the voice; the stamp is
    what would make it *believable*. *"The stamp is what turns a voice into the news"* is
    no longer a general observation — it is his problem in one line. **He never says this
    and must never say it.** A man who will not think about Chaac is not going to admit he
    wants a pulpit. Let it surface at the reckoning.
  - Note the trap it sets: **the more famous he becomes, the more visible a man with an
    unprovable secret becomes.** Being seen is the premise of the game and the worst thing
    that could happen to him. Day 25 is *Hunted* and Day 30 is *Captured* — that arc now
    has a cause instead of a plot requirement.
- **He keeps the count** — *"Only three others made it out"* … *"It was four, damn it."*
- **He swears when he is being honest**, and rarely enough that it lands.
- **He amends labels privately** — *"Ex-Nemean, I think to myself."* Never to a face.
  (style guide §2)
- **Cloak, cane, gun.** The cloak carries the emblem, not the body — which is what lets
  him take it off and give it to her. The cane is implant metal and a weapon. The
  las-pistol has a **recuperator** that is never explained on the page and exists only
  so that *las-pistol*, *cold steel*, *three recoils* and *burned-out craters* all stay
  consistent (punchlist §C).
- **One thing he cannot name** — *"something older underneath it that I haven't got a
  name for."* Do not spend it. (style guide §4)

## 5. Chip

- A **VTuber** off a failing adult-content page. Doesn't need the money so much as the
  *plot*.
- **She sells; she does not confess.** Her appeal is a **bill**, not a request for
  sympathy — *"you'll abandon poor Chipper on the street corners."* Round numbers,
  third person (*"your oshi"*), addressed to a *you*. The exaggeration is her
  **dignity**: she plays broke rather than being broke, so she stays in control of the
  transaction. The act is the labour.
- **Her subscriber count is a licence.** The Feed issues it and can revoke it. She and
  the narrator want the same kind of object from different issuers — his tick, her subs.
  Hers is the one that works.
- **She is his Sancho.** She has no legitimacy problem and no morality problem; she has
  a goal and a deadline. She believes in donations, not in anything else.
- **She is his mirror in rudeness.** Same species of provocation, opposite intent: hers
  is aimed, his is involuntary.
- Family: an older sister, close. **They disowned their father.** Basics like a car and
  health insurance are out of reach for her generation; *"bullets feel easier than
  stalkers."*

## 6. The Three — Chaac Patera

**The count.** Three others made it out of the verdigris hellpits of Chaac Patera, plus
the narrator. One of them was dead three weeks later. So **four landed, one is dead,
three are alive.**

**Chaac is also public knowledge** — a byword for a massacre, not a secret. The recruiter
goes solemn at the name because he knows what happened there. He is sorry, not suspicious,
and that distinction is the whole chapter's misdirection: the mundane explanation arrives
from a decent man, which is why the reader believes it.

**The chant.** `"THE THREE RETURNED!"` — a dive-bar chant, sung by lads who were never
there. It counts the **living**, not the landing, and it lands on three by accident. It
is a **slogan**, and a slogan outlives its census: nobody in the room cares which three,
or whether the number was ever right.

**What the number is actually for.** It refers to the **old myth**, not to a manifest.
Three is a ritually loaded number, and this is the kind of thing that attaches itself to
a myth and stays attached long after the facts move on. The chant is older than the men
it is about.

**Rules.**
- **Never SETTLE the count for the reader.** Deadpan arithmetic — a private census that
  quietly squares the bar against the manifest — spends a joke the reader could have
  made themselves. The slogan is allowed to be approximate, and his flatness about it is
  what makes it land.
- **A flare is not a correction.** He may *fumble at* the number and then give it up in
  the same breath, out loud: *"It was four, damn it. / But never let the numbers fuck up
  a good chant."* That is complicity rather than correction — he chooses the myth
  knowingly, which is exactly the instinct that will let the visor fool him. **The rule
  is *no census*, not *no temper*.**
- **The myth, if it is ever named, is named once, as a proper noun, and never
  explained** — style guide §4.
- **There is no spare survivor.** The two are load-bearing: one is the **moon-touched
  soldier** (*"They're not bugs!"*), one is the **Complicit / Whistleblower** fork.

## 7. Money, numbers and the goal convention

- **The platform takes 80%.** *"Hitting 1,000 subs unlocks paid hyperchats, minus an 80%
  platform cut."* This is why the goals are survival numbers, not riches.
- **The ladder:** 1,000 subs unlocks hyperchats and rents a studio bunker (hype
  multiplier up) → 5,000 subs buys body armour → 10,000 subs buys real polycarbon /
  the 4K camera.
- **The five-day tax is COLONY-WIDE.** Everyone pays it. Chip's *"kill the taxman"*
  goal is her **monetising an ordinary bill by putting a health bar on it** — she does
  not complain about the tax, she turns it into content. (Doc: "$10,000 tax goal every
  five days.")
- **Number convention: ROUND FOR MILESTONES, LUMPY FOR BILLS.** Milestones are round in
  reality — that is what a milestone *is* — so 1,000 / 5,000 / 10,000 subs stay round.
  Bills are never round, so the engine and the taxman take lumpy figures. This also
  stops the collisions: as written, 5,000 is both a sub goal and the engine, and 10,000
  is both a sub goal and the tax.
- **The tax is the number you WANT to move.** A figure that changes is more memorable
  than one that doesn't, and the Ministry raising the levy is the difficulty curve made
  of story.
- **Money slang: *dust*.** The everyday coin — pocket money, the payment for a small thing. A mass
  noun: *a hundred dust*, *no dust*, *that's dust*. It sits under **the water**, which is money at
  scale (§3). The ground produces the word twice over: the tide spits realgar dust, and a crater
  becomes a claim. *Chips* stays the physical coin — the metal in a pocket.
- **Money slang: *cake*.** The other money — platform money, the soft kind: dome food, topside money,
  what the Feed pays out. *That's cake* means *easy* as well as *paid*, and the double is free,
  because for the people taking it those are the same thing. **Cake is hers; dust is his.**

## 9. Places

- **Io** — the battlefield. Loki Patera, the largest caldera. Sulfur stains it
  yellow-green, eruptions red. A **patera** is a broad shallow depression, and
  settlements sit on the **rims**. Terraforming "Pipers"/"Smoketops" thickened the air:
  warm enough to live, and it blinds and poisons the surface.
- **Juno** — the new capital, and the home of **Keno's**.
- **Argos** — the old capital, and the first place "infected". **Held back.** It is a
  *place*, not a fact, and it pays off when somebody goes there.
- **Chaac Patera** — where the narrator was maimed. Verdigris hellpits.
- **Keno's** — best dive bar in the new capital. Technically *the White Cow*, named for
  the myth: Io, Jupiter's harem-girl, hidden as a cow from Juno's jealousy. Everyone
  calls it after **Captain Keno**, who has never raised his prices.

### Hot springs

Where ***the Kiss*** lands. The payload does not flash off — it pools. Over time, in the
right place, it becomes a spring. Imported Europa, sitting in a hole in Io.

Three conditions, and they are why springs are rare rather than everywhere:

1. **Pressure.** The Pipers' thickened air sits above water's triple point. That is the
   only reason liquid water can exist on Io at all.
2. **Heat.** Io's own volcanism. A pool in cold crust is ice, not a spring. Springs sit in
   **caves**, on **calderas**, and around the **Pipers**.
3. **Purity.** No sulfur. The whole crust is stained yellow-green with it, so a spring
   that never touches a fumarole is the freak case, not the rule. *Pure* means the air
   and the ground, not the water.

**Three grades, and the grade is a class marker:**

- **Cave springs** — the common safe water. Undetectable from orbit, which matters.
- **The baths** — artificial, certified, tourist-facing, sited on the Pipers. Stable,
  expensive, and the pleasure of the rich. This is *assured water* with a room around it:
  the same product the Ministry sells at the tap, sold a second time as leisure.
- **Open-air springs** — rumoured, seasonal, occasionally permanent, whispered along the
  surface trade routes. **No confirmed permanent one.** Seasonality is mechanical: the
  Kiss lands where the alignment sends it, so a spring fed by one landing dries out until
  the orbits bring the payload back.

### Settlement and the fringe

**The predictable landing sites are the frontier.** Where the Kiss is known to come down,
you get people: frontier colonies, bandit strongholds, Ministry stations, and towns that
traded protection for obedience. Some are independent, some allied, some older than the
war itself.

Beyond them, on a moon that looks from orbit like a dead and dangerous planet, there is
**fringe life of every kind**: hippie communes, monasteries, strange science cults, and
Silk Road settlements strung along the surface trade routes. There are rare hardy
individuals out here on purpose, and they are not all running from something.

**Staging rule of thumb: the further from the Ministry, the less dome.** Sealed domes near
the capital, respirators and jointed shacks on the fringe, open air for the people who
have decided the air is survivable enough.

### Ice pillars

The Kiss arrives as **ice**, not as a slug of water. A mass driver can put a block on Io
for almost nothing; what it cannot do is put it down gently.

**Where the reader learns it.** The intro's narration used to carry the trade — the 3.5-day orbit,
the mass drivers, the tonnage. Cut from there on purpose: it belongs *here*, the first time a pillar
is witnessed on the field. What the bar keeps is the **name** and nothing else — *the Kiss* on a card
as an ingredient, *"Hump it"* from a customer, *"the scrambler"* in the narrator's mouth — all
unglossed, which is fine. He knows what they mean and never explains.

**Speed decides what you get.**
- **Fast delivery** — the block flashes on impact. That is the *kiss-flash*, the most
  filmable and most dangerous weather on Io.
- **Slow delivery** — a lower launch velocity means a longer transit and an intact
  landing. That is a **pillar**: a standing column of imported Europa, waiting.

**Pillars do not melt on their own.** Io's surface runs around -130 °C away from volcanic
ground, so a pillar on cold crust is architecture, not a spring. It melts where the ground
is hot: calderas, fumaroles, Piper exhaust. Which gives two kinds of pillar — **permanent
ice in the cold, and a spring in slow motion in the hot.**

**And a pillar field is water as real estate.** A pillar is recoverable water sitting out
in the open, so it is claimed, surveyed, and entered in a ledger. Therefore:

> **A spring is a pillar that got away** — melt that left the ledger before anybody filed
> on it.

That is the supply side of the three spring grades: the baths are melt the Ministry
metered, cave water is melt that ran underground, and an open-air rarity is a pillar that
melted somewhere nobody had claimed.

**Delivery is by mass, not by aim.** The drivers throw tonnage; they do not place it. So
one throw scatters: some blocks flash off, some crack into fragments, some land whole,
and some leave a crater. Nothing arrives where it was aimed.

- **In cold ground the fragments are preserved.** That is where the water actually is —
  not in a depot but in a **field**: ice shards, half-buried blocks, yellow-dusted and
  reading as rock at a glance.
- So the geography of the colony in one line: **the water is stored where nobody can
  live, and the living happens where water cannot stay.** Around -130 °C out on the cold
  crust the ice keeps more or less forever; in the warm zones it melts, floods, or
  evaporates.
- **A fresh crater is a new claim,** and a crater that lands on a settlement is a
  shipping error. This is the strongest thing the element grants: the logistics arm's
  ordinary, innocent function is *also* its weapon, and intent is unprovable.
- Gathering it is brute labour in the worst real estate on the moon, which is what
  *the pass* is for.

**Hazards — reuse the existing colour language, do not add a fourth:**

- **Orange — falling.** A block coming in overhead is an *overhead near-miss*.
- **Yellow — ground.** A preserved shard field, or the lip of a fresh crater, is a
  *car-hitting* obstacle. It is a **disguised** hazard, because the sulfur dust makes the
  ice read as stone — the thing you need looks like the thing you are standing on.
- **A collapsing pillar** is a set piece and counts as both.

**For staging:**
- Pillars are dusted with sulfur, so they are **yellow-white, not blue-white**.
- They are **visible from orbit** — which makes them the exact opposite of a spring. A
  pillar field is content and a ratings opportunity; a cave spring is the one place a
  camera cannot follow. The team will want to film one, and Aysel will claim she has.
- A collapse is an event. A pillar coming down is a set piece, and it is *on camera*.
- Pillars and springs share one precondition: an atmosphere above water's triple point.
  If that ever stops being true, both stop existing.

**Why the springs matter more than water:**

- **They are off-camera.** A cave has no sky and the Eye cannot see into it (style guide
  §7). The springs are therefore the only places on Io where the narrator is *not* being
  filmed — the one place a person can be a person. He will care about that more than he
  cares about the water.
- **They are free, and therefore dangerous.** The Ministry sells *certification* of
  water. A spring is water that needs no certification, so it is a political problem, and
  the Ministry's answer is already written: the rumour that *it is the water* points
  straight at the springs.
- **A permanent open-air spring is the most valuable thing on Io** — an unlicensed
  paradise. Its location is currency, and it gives the endings somewhere to aim.

## 10. Props and production

- **The glass badge of an eye** — the Civilian War Journalism License. A man whose
  belief system is *rightful* authority is handed **glass**. It sits immediately above
  *"I wanted a name on mine."*
- **The las-pistol and the recuperator** — see §4. Do not explain it.
- **The bench in the slums** — the same bench supplies his leg *and* his water.
- **The prosthesis** — the VA replaces it every four years, *in the version they
  approved, at the price they set*. The good version came off the bench.
- **The vehicle** — a government logistics-arm propaganda van, customised by Chip. The
  roof hatch lifts her into a roof cage: **the ring**. She owns the roof and the paint,
  he owns the hull and the gun. Paint states track the campaign (Day 1 bare grey → Day 3
  wrap → Day 20 cover-up → Day 25 stripped and anonymous).
- **The engineer — working name "Tank"** (provisional). The prosthetic vendor whose upgrade
  the player refuses. Female, cold palette (steel, off-white, charcoal, teal) against Chip's
  warm pink and yellow. Teal hose, oversized welding torch. Round **toothed pauldrons** — the
  "horns" the visor misreads.
  - **Augmentations (decided):** a **simple prosthetic leg**, and a **mechanical hand** worn
    under a loose work glove. The glove is gear, not a prosthesis — which is useful, because
    the hand can be *hidden* and revealed the moment she takes the glove off. That is a scene
    you get for free.
  - **Plain glass goggles. NEVER an AR visor.** Both new concept sheets drifted into a glowing
    cyan optics rig, and this is the most important thing to correct, because the rule is not
    aesthetic. **She is the only character with clean eyes.** Give her a HUD and she sees the
    same false world as everyone else, and the story loses its one pair of eyes that can tell
    the truth. Cyan is also the *camera's* colour in the slice's own language — filmer
    targeting — so a cyan visor puts the Feed's mark on the one person who should carry none
    of it.
  - **The three-way prosthetic hierarchy**, which is the triad rendered as a limb:

    | limb | who | standing |
    |---|---|---|
    | the authorised leg | the narrator | legitimate, expensive, on a four-year cycle, and it did not fix him |
    | the simple leg | the engineer | hers by choice, plain, unjointed, and it works |
    | the graft | what she offers him | the upgrade, refused |

    **0.18 g is why a simple leg is viable at all** — falls are soft, so a rigid unjointed
    prosthetic is survivable here in a way it would not be on Earth.
  - **She is already cybernetic**, so the visor would render *her* as a Sectoid too. That is
    exactly why she is the right person to make the case the narrator refuses.
  - **The pauldron has to taper before it can become a horn.** Both sheets draw it as a round
    toothed disc with a bolt ring — excellent gear, but *flat*, so the HUD has nothing conical
    to misread. Give it a projecting, tapering form (the hose fitting flaring out, or a curved
    exhaust) and keep the gear reading underneath. Gear first, horn second.
  - **COLLISION:** "tank" is already the vehicle class — `Enemy models/`, and README.md lists
    "drone, buggy, tank". In a game with vehicle combat, a person called Tank and an enemy
    called a tank will collide in every line of dialogue and every spec. Keep it as a
    file/asset name for now; settle the spoken name in the scene where she is introduced.
    (Where it came from: the sheets contain both a black tank top and a gas canister on her
    belt. The working name describes her *gear*, not her.)
- **The prologue** — one painted plate, the camera does the work. `leg_pool`, `stomp`,
  `black`. `arm_crawl` and `grab_leg` were cut, and their assets deleted.

## 11. The campaign

- **40 in-game days**, scrappy start-up to fugitive, **checkpoints every five days**.
- **Core loop:** choose a route (easy / medium / hard) → vehicle combat mission → shop →
  canteen or evening scene.
- **Stats:** Hype, Sympathy, Bloodlust, Drama, **Patriotism**.
- **Shop lines:** personal gear (armour, lithium, alcohol), streaming gear (lens, gyro,

## 12. Physics — the numbers, so nothing contradicts

- **Surface gravity: 0.183 g.** About one fifth of Earth, a shade over the Moon's 0.166.
- **The ground is a tide.** Jupiter flexes the solid surface by up to roughly **100 m**. Io is
  tidally locked, so the bulge sits at a **fixed** spot on the surface and only the *amplitude*
  swells — **one peak per 42.5-hour orbit**, called **perijove** (closest approach; **apojove**
  is the far end). Because the bulge does not travel, a *fixed* spire has to be a **stalled
  tectonic uplift** — and that is what real Io's mountains are: Boösaule Montes is about 17.5 km
  and is not a volcano. Hence the Steeple's height varies over the orbit, which the doc already
  implies with "3–4.3 miles".
  - **What it is called.** The worker's word is **a land tide**. The Ministry's word is
    **the Settling** — a press-office name for a hundred metres of ground moving twice a
    day, in the same family as *the Revelation* (style guide §5). The scientific vocabulary
    stays in the notes: *body tide*, *tidal flexure*, *tidal heating*, *Love number*.
  - **At real scale you cannot see it.** 100 m against a radius of 1,821 km is a slope of
    about 0.003° — invisible, and you rise with the ground, so there is no relative motion
    to notice either. **The body tide is a schedule, not a spectacle.** It is legible only
    through what it does: pools re-levelling, long straight runs failing at their joints,
    fresh scarps opening, and the landform it has raised over centuries. You see the tide
    the way you see wind — in what it made.
  - **The prop that makes it present indoors: a tide clock.** As ordinary as a clock, in
    every public room, and nobody looks at it. One belongs beside the Feed screen in
    Keno's: two instruments, what to think about and what to be afraid of,
    and *who watches which* is characterisation for free.
  - **DECIDED: the tide is a slow rise, and the ripples are events.** The baseline flexure is
    the invisible ~100 m above — a schedule nobody watches. On top of it the crust **rolls**:
    wave trains a few times a day, worst at perijove, and a bad one parts the raft and glows.
    - **The mechanism is real.** Io's Love number implies a global magma ocean at shallow
      depth, so the crust is a **raft on a liquid**, and visible ground can heave like a sheet
      on a swell. The folk vocabulary therefore turns **nautical**:
      *a set*, *the swell*, *a sea*, *the roll*. On a moon with no water, the only sea
      anyone knows is the floor.
    - **What makes it visible is WAVELENGTH, not amplitude.** 100 m over a 1–2 km swell is
      3–6° of tilt — enough to read on the horizon, to tip a vehicle, to spill a glass.
      "Exaggerated" means *shorter waves*, not bigger numbers.
    - **Feet first.** Seismic waves arrive before the dust does, so the order is: vibration,
      then the horizon dips and rises, then the dust front comes across the plain.
    - **Architecture becomes mooring.** If the ground rolls, nothing gets *founded* — things
      sit on bearings, cables and flexible mounts. **A building on Io is a boat tied to a
      dock.** That is what the joints are for: not a slow rise, but shock.
    - **And a bad one opens the floor.** The raft parts and what is underneath is molten.
      Which is what a *patera* is — a place the raft has already parted — and why settlements
      sit on the **rims**: the rim is the high ground.
    - **The clock and the alarm.** The tide clock exists because the tide is invisible. The
      ripple is why nobody entirely trusts it: the clock says when to be afraid, and the
      ground says when the clock was wrong.
    - **The words for the peak.** *Perijove* is the **official** term — astronomical, neutral,
      and the same Latinate family as *the Revelation* and *the Kiss*. The worker's word for
      the same moment is **high water**, which sits in the nautical set above and keeps the
      paradox: on a moon with no water, high water means the ground.
    - **And the calendar already resonates — the existing numbers line up.** Io's period is
      1.769 days, Europa's 3.551 — the 2:1 Laplace resonance, so Io laps Europa once per Europa
      orbit. The doc's "every 3.5 days their orbits are closest" **is Europa's period**, which
      puts the Kiss launch every **second** Io orbit: *two tides, then the water.* So the tide
      table and the launch table are the same table, and the springs pulse on the water beat.
    - **Three clocks, all predictable, all expensive:** the tide at **42.5 h**, the water at
      **3.5 d**, the tax at **5 d**. Only the last one is in the game so far, which is a great
      deal of rhythm sitting unused.
    - **And perijove is knowable to the minute, which is the trap.** Keplerian, tabulated,
      forecast like weather. The *timing* is certain; the *behaviour* is not. Knowing when the
      swell peaks tells you when to be afraid and nothing about whether the raft will part.
    - Keep the flat ~100 m figure in the notes as the number the Ministry quotes.
- **Heat flux is enormous** — on the order of **2.5 W/m²**, some thirty times Earth's
  average. The ground is a radiator. A cave is warm near a patera and merely sheltered
  away from one, and **beside a lava lake the engineering problem is cooling, not cold.**
- **You do not float at 0.183 g.** You walk normally, with weight, and about a fifth of
  it. The feel is not floaty, it is **slick**:
  - **Falls are soft.** A 2 m drop lands at ~2.7 m/s — stepping off a low kerb on Earth.
    A 10 m drop lands at ~6 m/s, like a 1.8 m fall. **The lethal thing on Io is the
    chemistry, not the drop.**
  - **Traction is the problem.** A fifth of your weight is a fifth of your friction. You
    do not fall, you **slide** — downhill walking is genuinely dangerous and braking is
    the hard part of every vehicle.
  - **Momentum is cheap; stopping is expensive.** Thrown distances run about five times
    Earth's, objects arc forever, and a lever does absurd work — part of why the cane is
    a weapon.
  - **Recoil is a mobility problem, not a comfort problem.** The same impulse shoves you
    about five times as far, which is a *reason* the recuperator exists.
- **Raised here means built here.** Bones, heart and circulation are set for 0.183 g, so
  the colonists cannot go home. That is the separatist motive, and it makes "escape home"
  a stranger promise than it sounds.

### The vertical (working model)

Three axes decide where anyone lives: **heat, pressure, and the tide.** Underground wins
on all three at once — no acid, no radiation, no flex — which is why it is the default and
why the majority live in caves. Note that heat is the *least* of the three reasons they
dig: a cave that keeps the poison out is already worth the dig.

- **Below ground** — the majority. Free shelter, warm near a patera, and no sky. So also
  the least observed.
- **Above ground, engineered** — sealed domes, greenhouse-lit, near-Earth conditions. The
  wealthy, who have bought the surface back.
- **Above ground, unengineered** — jointed shacks, respirators, and the fringe. Frontier
  towns, bandit strongholds, and the landing sites, because that is where the water comes
  down.

Which gives the class marker for free: **certified air, certified water, certified war.**
The Ministry sells all three, and the domes are the third product with a wall around it.
The rich buy the conditions; everyone else breathes the dawn.

## 13. The Authority and Albrecht

**The office.** **Grand Executor Albrecht** — chief of the government, which sits above the
Ministry of Logistics and Public Relations. The title is *Grand Executor*, not *Executor*: *Grand*
is real institutional usage (Grand Master, Grand Vizier, Grand Inquisitor), so the form is
authentic rather than pulp.

**The echo that earns the title: the Grand Inquisitor.** The motives rhyme. An authority whose
warrant he knows is unsound, held anyway for the sake of people he pities, argued rather than
enforced. The Inquisitor's instrument is the **argument**, he wants agreement rather than
obedience, and he gets none — a kiss and silence, and *"the old man adheres to his idea."*

**Miracle, mystery, authority** — the three powers the Inquisitor names as the price of peace.
All three already exist here: **the Feed** is the miracle, **the withheld cause** is the mystery
(*"the rumor is it's the water"*), **the licence** is the authority. That is the exchange rate the
story runs on. The stat list already carries **Patriotism**, so the recruiter's *"true Ionian
patriots"* has a system behind it.

**Division of labour, and it must hold: the recruiter quotes, Albrecht argues.** The clerk repeats
a doctrine he does not own — he reads it off the pamphlet. If the recruiter ever argues on his own
behalf there are two Grand Inquisitors and no cell.

**The narrator's silence is the correct form.** The prisoner answers with a gesture, never with
pressure; *"as I wait in silence"* is that form. In the intro his gesture is the cloak — the
emblem argues, the room goes quiet, nobody is converted. (This is the answer to punchlist E5's
*"make the silence pressure."*)

**Give him one scene of self-justification or the title is decoration** — a man defending an
indefensible thing to somebody who will not answer. The slot already exists: *"Let it surface at
the reckoning."*

**Open — who says which form.** Proposed rule: **the full title is paper, deference, or devotion**
(*Grand Executor* on a document; also from a man who wants something from him; also from an
idolater) and **the short form is a man at work** (*the Executor*, from a clerk doing his job).
The **narrator**, being a legitimist who reaches for the big word, uses the full title — he
believes in the ladder. The intro now has the **recruiter** saying *"Grand Executor Albrecht"*, i.e.
the full form, so he is deferring or devoted rather than a man doing his job. Decide which he is.
The narrator's own form is still unspent: he never says the name.

**Proposed, not settled.**

- **The government's name: the Authority.** It rhymes with the licence, which grants *authority*,
  and it follows the naming system — the Ministry, the Feed, the Kiss. The bare form is also right
  for an entity whose problem is *being* one: canon has the Ministry selling the licence that
  authenticates the war, so the Authority is the buyer of its own legitimacy.
- **The Ministry's collection arm: the Contribution.** The historical word for a levy paid to be
  spared — i.e. the five-day tax — and it sounds voluntary while being extracted. Device:
  *Der Krieg ernährt den Krieg*, **the war feeds the war** (the phrase attached to Wallenstein's
  contribution system). German on the seal, English in the mouth; the seal is inherited paper in a
  language nobody on Io reads, so it decorates and explains nothing.
- **The previous administration was the Giant Leap Pioneer Project** — a chartered development
  project, not a government. No citizens, only claimants and contract-holders; law by contract;
  administration organised by deliverable. When the dream popped, nothing replaced it, so nobody
  was left holding clean title. That vacuum is why the licence exists at all.

## 14. The flight, and the Metamorphosis

**The menu.** Keno's house flight is **the four jewels of Io**, drunk in myth order — **the White
Cow, the Peacock, the Gadfly, the Metamorphosis**. The name is stated once, at the order, and never
pointed at again. It pays off in the catalogue, where each drink is described as a stone —
milkstone, sapphire, opal, peridot, topaz — so the last one arrives as its own title.

**The Metamorphosis is a water drink** — absinthe's service with a different cause. A topaz-specked
syrup in a **Pontarlier** (stemmed, thick pinched bulb, dose line cut in it), served with *a spoon,
sugar, and the Kiss in a scrambler*. The trigger is the imported water, under the same name the
trade uses, on a card, with no gloss.

**Two results, and they are the thesis:**

| pour | the drink | the point |
|---|---|---|
| right — slow, through the sugar | one flat colour, *"flat as snowy Europa"* | the method reproduces the **source**. Nothing is created; you are shown the moon the water came from |
| wrong — dumped in | topaz → emerald → rivers of blue → white cloud → clear | the **destination**: land, sea, sky in order, and then gone |

*Snowball Earth* is the real term for a frozen world, and it is the read on the first row.
Never say it (§4).

**The mechanism, and it is why the ritual matters.** A slow drip through sugar makes a fine, even
emulsion and one colour. Dumped fast, the emulsion goes coarse, the phases separate, and each
colourant blooms on its own before it settles. So the procedure is the Ministry's method and the
method is the sell: follow the steps, get a snowball; ignore them, get a world — for four seconds,
in a glass, at the price of a round.

**It ends clear, on Chip's reflection, and that is the point.** The recruiter sells *a real sky*;
the drink performs it nightly and drains it. The water caused nothing, and what is left is a girl
who got the whole thing by accident.

**Keno's reaction is the plant.** He lifts the scrambler, looks at what's left, and sets it back —
no interiority, no gear-turning; the narrator infers from acts and never asks. He will eventually
sell a drink named after her.

**Rejected: the Albrecht Effect.** Naming the effect after the Executor was considered and dropped.
The press-office naming is already the Ministry's; the warm half of the same impulse belongs to
Keno, at his own scale, and that is the one worth having.

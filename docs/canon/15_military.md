# Canon §15 — The military: ranks, the reform, the new order

Part of War Idol canon: detailed rulings a scene must obey. Index: `canon.md`.
The story bible owns the big picture and **wins any conflict**. Albrecht's backstory is in the
bible, "Albrecht"; his title and voice are in §13.

---

**Two layers of naming.** Io's military has **always used Roman (Latin) forms**. **Albrecht's
reform moved it to Byzantine (Greek) forms.** The precedent is Heraclius in the 7th century: the
empire rebuilt itself after a near-collapse and moved from Latin to Greek, so Rome became Byzantium
partly by changing language in a crisis.

**Why Latin in the first place.** The pioneers (§13) were settlements that each did their own thing,
like Plymouth Colony. As population and complexity grew, a colonial government formed, distinct
from Earth's, and the Latin forms were one of its **deliberate anachronisms**: a way to distance
itself from Terran convention by reaching back to the old ages, inspired by the myths and the names
of the planets.

**The reform.** Marian in kind, Komnenian in its titles: a professional core loyal to Albrecht
personally, standardised kit built around **the visor**, and new titles. He came up through
logistics, which means pay and supply, and that is what the soldiers' loyalty runs through.

**A year zero nobody declares.** Augustus, not the French Revolution. Coin, dates, measures, titles
and the capital (Juno) are replaced quietly until the old order is hard to remember. **Never
announce it on the page.** It shows in texture: prices in two currencies, veterans converting old
measures in their heads, a document dated by the old reckoning, old hands saying *centurion*.
- Restoring the Terpsichore properties (§8) means restoring them **inside his system**: his coin,
  his titles. The return of what was hers is also how it is absorbed.
- **Street slang survives the reform.** *Dust*, *chips* and *cake* (§7) are untouched; the reform
  renames the official coin, not the street's words.

**Language is allegiance.** A Latin term marks the old guard or a pre-reform veteran; a Greek term
marks the reformed army and the Ministry. Which word a character uses tells you which side they
came from.

**Working mapping, old → new** (a target, not final):

| old (Latin) | new (Greek) | roughly |
|---|---|---|
| Legatus | Strategos | general |
| Legio | Thema / Tagma | provincial army / guard regiment |
| Cohors | Tourma / Droungos | ~1,000–3,000 |
| Centurio | Kentarchos | commands ~100 |
| Optio | Pentekontarches | second in command, ~50 |
| Decanus | Dekarchos | commands ~10 |
| Contubernium | Kontoubernion / Dekarchia | the tent group, ~8–10 |
| Miles | Stratiotes | soldier |
| Clibanarii | Klibanophoroi | heavy exosuit troops |

- Old titles may linger with new jobs. In Byzantium the *optio* survived as a paymaster or
  quartermaster. A messy reform is a believable one.

**Klibanophoroi** ("oven-wearers") are the heavy exosuit troops. Before the reform they were the
**clibanarii**, the real late-Roman heavy cavalry; the Greek word is the same unit renamed.

**The cheirosiphon** ("hand-siphon", the Byzantine hand-held Greek-fire projector) is the army's
armoured fighting vehicle, crew of four: driver, comms, leader, gunner. Crews say **Cheiro**.
Nobody on Io says *tank*; it is an old Earth word (which is why the engineer's name is a joke,
§10). *Decided 2026-11-15.*

**At Chaac (decided 2026-11-15).** The narrator was the leader of a cheirosiphon crew in the
**Nemean Cohort**; his crew call him **Dek** (from *dekarchos*; *Archos* is the fallback if
"Dek" reads as a name). The order that drove his Cheiro into the landtide was his — see
`chaac_run_spec.md`, and §4: the page never says so in his voice.

**Readability on the page.** Where a unit name is spoken, prefer the form a player can read
(*Nemean Cohort*). The Greek forms (*Tourma*, *Droungos*…) are world-building for the
**dossiers**, not for dialogue.

**The narrator is a dekarchos marked for promotion to kentarchos** — in Roman terms an *optio ad
spem ordinis*, "optio in hope of the rank". He keeps dodging it: he would rather fight than think,
and he would rather keep his small unit. A kentarchos running a contubernium-sized crew is the
other reading, and it is not this one.
- Dodging promotion is of a piece with not looking at the bigger picture (§4). **Never say so on
  the page.**

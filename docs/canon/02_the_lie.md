# Canon §2 — The lie — rules for writing it

Part of War Idol canon: detailed rulings a scene must obey. Index: `canon.md`.
The story bible owns the big picture and **wins any conflict**.

---

The premise — no pathogen, no alien, ordinary humans in work gear, the visor makes the
monster, the audio and hitbox tells — is in the **story bible, "The lie"**. This file holds
the rules for writing it.

**What is real**
- The colonists are **heavily equipped and often cybernetic**, for practical reasons — Io
  is a hard-rock industrial colony. The suits, the limbs, the gear are real. (Why that is
  not a moral failing: style guide §5 on euphemism, and the note that "purity of the body"
  is a bourgeois concern.)
- A **military junta** holds Io under martial law, and it wants the moon.
- A **separatist** movement exists and fights.

**Why the reader is not cheated.** "It was all a rendering" only feels like a cheat if the
book hid its hand. This one shows it twice over: the **tells** in his vocabulary (a body part
or a machine part that belongs to no bug — the prologue's *servos* and *toenails*), and the
**phantom pain** that flares whenever he gets close (§4). Everything the reader was told is
what the HUD showed him; the clues to the man underneath were on the page.

Consequences, and they bind every scene:
- **Nobody has ever seen a beast with their own eyes.** Every sighting is through
  Ministry hardware. If a character claims otherwise, it is a lie or a broken visor,
  and either way it is a scene.
- **The horns are a HUD artefact.** The engineer's round, toothed pauldrons are the
  standing example of what the visor misreads.
- **The prose describes the render**, because that is what he sees. Monster vocabulary
  is honest reporting of the HUD, not authorial deceit. The leaks are the human words.
- **"No living colonist has been found"** (Langley, Day 3) is not a mystery. It is the
  proof. The colonists are the aliens.
- The war is therefore a **counterinsurgency sold as a xenocide**, and
  *the Revelation* is a manufactured apocalypse — which is why the Feed sells
  *the Apocalypse*.

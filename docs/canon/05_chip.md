# Canon §5 — Chip

Part of War Idol canon: detailed rulings a scene must obey. Index: `canon.md`.
The story bible owns the big picture and **wins any conflict**.

---

- A **VTuber** off a failing adult-content page. Doesn't need the money so much as the
  *plot*.
- **She sells; she does not confess.** Her appeal is a **bill**, not a request for
  sympathy — *"you'll abandon poor Chipper on the street corners."* Round numbers,
  third person (*"your oshi"*), addressed to a *you*. The exaggeration is her
  **dignity**: she plays broke rather than being broke, so she stays in control of the
  transaction. The act is the labour.
- **Her subscriber count is a licence.** The Feed issues it and can revoke it. She and
  the narrator want the same kind of object from different issuers — his tick, her subs.
  Hers is the one that works.
- **She is his Sancho.** She has no legitimacy problem and no morality problem; she has
  a goal and a deadline. She believes in donations, not in anything else.
- **She is his mirror in rudeness.** Same species of provocation, opposite intent: hers
  is aimed, his is involuntary.
- Family: an older sister, close. **They disowned their father.** Basics like a car and
  health insurance are out of reach for her generation; *"bullets feel easier than
  stalkers."*
- **The concert and the fan kid** (added 2026-11-17; prose in `docs/stories/Chip's Concert.txt`).
  At a live show (cherry-blossom light-sticks, the "Give us the War Idol!" chant) a boy
  vaults the ropes in a costume too big for him: an **E-2** who quit school after seeing
  her on the Feed, a **gold member** whose super chats come as **D4rK-P4l1id4n**. Her
  videos put him in uniform. It lands on her like a punch, and the filters edit out her
  breathing; she stays **pro first, big sister last** — promises him a song request for
  Saturday, then mutes her mic to whisper the one line the crowd never hears: *"Stay alive,
  okay? Or else Chip is going to make fun of you forever."* The narrator films it and tells
  himself the boy is old enough. This is the cost of her act made concrete: the bill she
  sells is paid by recruits.
